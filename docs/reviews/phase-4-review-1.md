## I. The legal and licence claims (task 2 in full)

- **Key blob:** the plan's finding is right in every detail (section A), and P9 removes it. Missing: ClCertA (B1).
- **Is P10(a) a sound way to meet GPLv3's corresponding-source duty? Yes.** GPLv3 section 1 defines Corresponding
  Source as the source "needed to generate, install, and (for an executable work) run the object code and to modify
  the work", plus the build scripts. A file the build does not read is not part of it, so leaving it out keeps the
  source complete; releasing modified source is allowed under section 5 with a prominent notice and date (SOURCE.md,
  plus N6 inside the tarball). The proof is good for a header: clang's `-MD` lists every file the preprocessor
  opened; the only CMake reference is `src/core/CMakeLists.txt:502` under the OFF option; the header `#error`s if
  included (:7-9). What the `.o.d` files cannot show: files read by CMake itself or by custom commands; for
  `default_keys.h` the grep shows none. S1 makes the proof unnecessary (the binary is built from the stripped tree).
  The patches and `build-core.sh` complete the source (the build-files asset and the tag). Same-page hosting meets
  section 6(d)'s "equivalent access ... through the same place".
- **(b):** lawful under GPLv3 6(d) with the standing duty above; weaker in practice (third-party availability) and
  in policy (it points at the keys).
- **LibreSSL's advertising clause with GPLv3 here:** on the licence texts, incompatible: the FSF says so; the OpenSSL
  licence adds clauses 3 and 6 and the SSLeay licence refuses relicensing under the GPL (COPYING:127-131); GPLv3
  section 7 lets a recipient drop "further restrictions", and section 10 forbids imposing them; the System Library
  exception does not reach a bundled, statically linked LibreSSL. In practice Azahar and Citra ship this combination
  in every release, with no OpenSSL exception in their licence, and faad2's third-party holder forbids non-GPL use.
  **Only a lawyer can decide** whether that practice gives enough implied permission for us to redistribute, and
  how much exposure a non-commercial redistributor has. The engineering exits are in S5. Including the
  acknowledgments in NOTICES meets the OpenSSL terms themselves; it does not cure the GPL side.
- **Bundled-licence facts:** see section A for each file and line; corrections in S6; the `src/` notices in S7.
- **Other lawyer-only points:** whether the HLE decryption code (present with or without keys) is "circumvention
  surface" for a public release (policy rule 7; DMCA 1201(a)(2), and Japan's Unfair Competition Prevention Act,
  whose 2018 amendment reaches the provision of circumvention codes and services); S4 shrinks that question. Whether
  re-hosting or linking to an encrypted Nintendo client key is trafficking (B1 removes it from what we ship and
  host). The system archives' provenance (risk 10): the generator repo's licence and where its font and word list
  come from.
